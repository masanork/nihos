# Tab5 C6 firmware update through P4 SDIO

2026-11-01. One physical OTA run on the previously identified Tab5. This is
an update and post-update diagnostic result, not a C6 flash readback, power
cycle, rollback, Wi-Fi/BLE qualification or iroh interoperability result.

## Candidate and preparation

- C6 application, based on the public Hosted 1.4.1 slave example with optional
  uptime/free-heap response fields: 1,079,120 bytes, SHA-256
  `0ab00183efb3149b568d0968877f1a7718f7c79817748a8e5c0417957cbf5e3b`.
  This is a separately built application, not a byte-identical reconstruction
  of the M5 factory application.
- P4 OTA fixture: SHA-256
  `79ccccdbc26582b5608e902657cce9e4009418fbe32cb81f53fac4f3093e79cc`.
  Its mapped C6 bytes were compared to the above app. This exact P4 image had
  previously rejected an invalid serial start token on the device.
- Before the update, a separate P4 fixture performed a full transfer to an
  in-memory responder while the physical SDIO stream rejected OTA RPC IDs;
  it then reconnected to the unchanged C6. See
  [the preflight and simulation record](2026-11-01-c6-ota-map-probe.md).
- The real run took the shared writer lock and an exclusive serial lease,
  checked the P4 identity/security and existing partition layout, and saved
  and verified all 16 MiB of P4 flash before installing the fixture. The P4
  image and embedded C6 candidate identities were rechecked before the token.
  C6 ROM downloader security and C6 full flash contents were not read.

## Physical result

The P4 reported the exact mapped C6 candidate hash, `NIHOS_C6_OTA_READY`, four
progress markers, and:

```text
NIHOS_C6_OTA_RESULT status=00000001 stage=00000008 error_code=00000000 acknowledged_bytes=00107750
NIHOS_C6_OTA_POST status=00000001 stage=00000006 last_command=00000035 last_interrupts=0000001c error_code=00000000 peer_chip=0000000d version_known=00000001 major=00000001 minor=00000004 patch=00000001 diag_known=00000001 diag_uptime=00000001 diag_heap=0003db70
```

The fixture sends one Begin, sequential Write requests of at most 384 bytes,
and End, without replaying an ambiguous request. `00107750` is 1,079,120
bytes. Following the End response and its reboot delay, the fixture reset and
reconnected to C6, received Hosted 1.4.1, and observed the new diagnostic
extension: uptime 1 second, free heap **252,784 bytes** at that instant.
This gives evidence of an SDIO OTA transfer and boot of an app exposing the new
feature. It does not independently verify the C6 flash bytes or flash-security
state. The number is a momentary total free heap, not a contiguous allocation or
peak-memory measurement.

The captured P4 log SHA-256 is
`f682925993556bedaabeef11bc41b09d3be572bb6614c21183a0e448a6693ff9`.
`tools/c6_ota_evidence.py` accepted its candidate identity, acknowledged length,
post-reboot version/diagnostics and recorded start token. The checker reports
P4-observed evidence and deliberately rejects simulation captures.

## P4 restoration and remaining boundaries

The previous P4 loader and affected application span were restored; esptool
verified the entire P4 16 MiB against the immediately preceding backup SHA-256
`0a21266492da4c6f3e643ce481de3b723948a3871d59216b5d3c53c4e3c80ee0`.
The restored P4 was warm-reset afterwards; its boot output and the C6 state
*after that final P4 reset* were not captured. The C6 application update was
intentional and was not rolled back.

Private records, including `capture.log`, `ota-evidence.json`, `restored.json`,
command logs and the P4 backup, remain in
`/private/tmp/nihos-c6-ota-valid-physical-20261101/`. The P4 backup is private
and is not committed. OTA interruption behavior, a second OTA update, cold
power cycle, new-firmware Wi-Fi/BLE, OTA rollback, and a working independent
C6 recovery path remain unverified. Do not infer production reliability or
safe unattended update behavior from this single run.
